Dog Vomiting After Thanksgiving? How Much Rice and Chicken to Feed

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Friday morning the kitchen still smells like roasting turkey, and there is a wet patch on the rug the size of a dinner plate. Your dog threw up once at 2 a.m. and again at six. Now he is at the back door asking to go out a third time.

The question is not whether rice is a good idea. It is how much of it, starting when, and how to tell a dog who ate 1,200 calories too many from a dog who needs a clinic today.

Quick answer: one vomit from a bright dog who is still drinking is a rug problem. Three vomits in 24 hours, blood in the vomit, or a dog who will not drink is a clinic problem. For the home case, skip the long fast and feed 2 parts cooked white rice to 1 part skinless chicken, sized at 1.2 times his resting energy requirement. For a 25 lb dog that is 2.4 cups of the mix, split into four meals. The Dog Calorie Calculator gives you the resting number in seconds.

One Vomit, or Three

Frequency is the first filter, and it is the one owners skip. One episode of recognizable dinner within six hours of eating usually means an overfilled stomach emptied itself, and that dog is fine by noon. Repeated bile or foam, hours apart, on an empty stomach, is a different event.

Then count. Three vomits in 24 hours earns an exam, as does any vomiting past 24 hours, a dog who refuses water, or a belly that hurts when you press it. Bloody vomit or a black, tarry stool skips the count and goes straight to the clinic.

Small breeds get less patience. A Yorkie with bloody diarrhea can drop 5 to 10 percent of body weight in a day. An exam runs $75 to $150 at a daytime practice; the emergency hospital charges $200 to $400 before treatment starts.

Why the 24-Hour Fast Is Out

The old instruction was to pull food for a full day and let the gut rest. Practice has moved off that. A 2011 pilot study in the Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine fed dogs with severe acute pancreatitis through an esophageal tube and found the gut tolerated it, which undercut the case for keeping it empty. A gut that is not working gets worse at not working.

Four patients still should not fast, and this is where home plans go wrong. Puppies under six months carry small glycogen stores and can slide into low blood sugar within 12 to 18 hours. Toy breeds under about 6 lb are close behind. A diabetic dog on insulin who skips a meal but takes the full dose is heading for a hypoglycemic emergency.

Everyone else does better on a short gap: four to six hours past the last vomit, then a quarter of the day's portion, then another quarter two hours later if it stays down.

The Two Numbers Behind the Portion

Eyeballing portions is how a recovering dog ends up with diarrhea again on day three. Two numbers settle it.

The first is his resting energy requirement: 70 times his weight in kilograms raised to the 0.75 power. A healthy dog eats that number times 1.6 or more. A dog coming off a night of vomiting needs about 1.2 times it, because he is lying around and because overfeeding an irritated gut restarts the cycle. For a 25 lb dog, 70 × 11.30.75 is 433 calories, and 1.2 times that is 519.

The second is what a cup of the mix costs. A cup of cooked white rice is about 205 calories, a cup of diced cooked skinless chicken breast about 231. Two parts rice to one part chicken by volume makes three cups at 641 calories, so a cup of finished mix runs 214. Divide 519 by 214 and the day is 2.4 cups.

The Bowl That Is Not Bland

Rice and chicken is bland only if nothing else goes into the bowl. The version most people build on Friday holds a scoop of dark meat, a splash of gravy for flavor, and a knob of butter to make it slide down. That bowl is a smaller copy of Thursday.

That bowl is 102 percent of the 519 calories a 25 lb dog needs for the whole recovery day, and 28 grams of the fat that put pressure on the pancreas in the first place. Keep the meat white, the skin off, the gravy boat on the counter. If a rich plate started this, the 72-hour pancreatitis watch runs on its own clock.

The Exit Takes Longer Than the Illness

Two days of the mix is the usual course. Getting back to kibble is where people rush, and rushing is why the diarrhea returns on day four. Move in quarters: three parts bland to one part regular on day three, half and half on day four, one part bland to three parts regular on day five. If the stool loosens at any step, back up one step and hold it two days.

Rice and chicken is not a balanced food. It is low in calcium and phosphorus, short on fat-soluble vitamins. A week is a bridge. A month is a deficiency.

What to Skip

Metronidazole used to be automatic. A 2019 randomized trial in the Journal of the American Animal Hospital Association compared it with a probiotic and with placebo in acute uncomplicated diarrhea and found no meaningful difference in time to recovery. It still earns its place in Giardia. It is not the default for one bad night.

Canned pumpkin is fiber, not a calorie plan. A tablespoon per 10 lb can firm a loose stool, and pie filling brings sugar and spices that make things worse. It does not replace the portion math above.

Human loperamide is the one to leave in the cabinet. Collies, Shelties, and any dog with the MDR1 mutation can have a severe neurological reaction to an ordinary human dose.

Keep the water down. A dog needs roughly an ounce per pound per day, and withholding it from an animal about to lose fluid for two days is backward. The Dog Water Intake Calculator gives you the target.

Rice and Chicken Questions

How much rice and chicken should I feed a 50 lb dog?

Four cups of the 2:1 mix across the day, which is one cup per meal at four meals, built on 873 recovery calories. Weigh the cooked rice once and you will stop guessing for the rest of the week.

Should I fast my dog for 24 hours after Thanksgiving?

No, and never for a puppy, a toy breed, or a dog on insulin. Four to six hours past the last vomit, then small meals, is the current approach. Fasting buys nothing and risks hypoglycemia in small patients.

How long can a dog stay on rice and chicken?

Two days of the mix plus four days of mixing back to regular food is the standard run. Past a week it is short on calcium, phosphorus, and fat-soluble vitamins, which makes it a deficiency rather than a recovery plan.

Write the Times Down

Put the four portions in small bowls before the day starts, keep the water where he can reach it, and note the time of every vomit. That log is worth more at the clinic than anything you can rebuild from memory.

The red lines do not move for a holiday weekend: vomiting that will not stop, blood in the vomit or a black stool, a hard and painful belly, or a dog who stops drinking. Call for those, and call tonight rather than waiting for Monday. If the vomiting followed a plate of scraps rather than a normal meal, read the calorie math on the holiday plate before you refeed. If his belly is deep and distended, the two-hour bloat rule comes first.
