Thanksgiving Guests, Open Suitcases, and Ibuprofen: The 44 mg/kg Problem

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 19, 2026 · 8 min read

One 200 mg Advil is 44 mg/kg for a ten-pound dog. That is a single tablet off a nightstand, and it lands just under the 50 mg/kg line where ibuprofen starts eating holes in the stomach lining. The dog will not look sick until Friday.

Thanksgiving is the week your house holds more medication than it does the other fifty-one. Not yours. Your own bottles are already on a shelf the dog cannot reach, because you solved that problem years ago. The new bottles arrive in a duffel bag on Wednesday afternoon and get set down on the guest room floor, which is exactly nose height.

Quick answer: The drug most likely to poison a dog this week is one a guest brought. A latched room for luggage and a lidded pill case handle most of the risk. If a bottle turns up chewed, work out the dose in mg/kg before deciding how worried to be, because tablet count means nothing without body weight. A 10 lb dog and a 60 lb dog eating the same Advil are in different clinical worlds. Our Ibuprofen Toxicity Calculator takes weight, tablet strength, and count and gives you the band.

The Guest Room Is the One Room You Never Dog-Proofed

Four adults, three suitcases, one air mattress, a shared bathroom. Somewhere in that pile is a seven-day pill organizer, which is not one dose but seven, laid out in a row like a candy tray. It is the most dangerous object a guest brings into a house with a dog, and it is almost never treated that way.

Two windows carry nearly all the risk. The first is the twenty minutes after arrival, when bags land in the hallway and everyone is hugging and nobody is watching the floor. The second is the next morning between 5 and 7, when the house is quiet, the guests are asleep, and the dog is the only one awake.

A latched door beats a closed door. Dogs push unlatched interior doors open with a nose. They do not work a hook-and-eye. If the guest room has no latch, move the bags into a bathroom or a closet instead of trusting a door that swings.

And the dog is not hunting for ibuprofen. He is hunting for something to chew, and a bottle that rattles inside a bag is a toy that smells like his people.

One Tablet, Two Dogs

The same tablet is a stomach ache for a Labrador and a kidney injury for a terrier. Weight is the whole argument, which is why the calculator opens with it.

Here are the published lines behind those bands. Below roughly 25 mg/kg, most dogs get nothing worse than a stomach ache. Around 50 mg/kg the gastric lining begins to ulcerate. Between 100 and 125 mg/kg the kidneys take damage, and past 400 mg/kg the nervous system joins in with tremors, unsteady walking, and seizures. Those figures come from the Merck Veterinary Manual, and they are the same thresholds the ibuprofen calculator works from.

The friction point changes with the dog. A 50 lb dog needs six 200 mg tablets to reach 53 mg/kg. A 10 lb dog needs one and a bit. Half a tablet, the dose a helpful guest might snap off for a limping dog, is still 22 mg/kg on a ten-pounder.

The Two-Hour Window and the 72-Hour Bloodwork

Ibuprofen peaks in the blood one to three hours after it goes down and clears with a half-life of three to six hours. Fast enough that the useful interventions expire quickly. Slow enough that owners talk themselves into waiting.

Hours after ingestion What is happening What you do
0 to 30 minutesStill in the stomach; little absorbed yetCall poison control. Do not induce vomiting yourself
30 minutes to 2 hoursAbsorbing; blood levels climbing toward peakThe window where a clinic can still empty the stomach and give charcoal
2 to 6 hoursVomiting, drooling, tender abdomenGet seen. IV fluids protect kidney blood flow
6 to 24 hoursUlcers forming; the dog may look brighterStomach protectants, bland food, no human pain relievers
24 to 72 hoursCreatinine climbing, often with no outward signRecheck bloodwork even if the dog came home

The back end is the part that surprises people. Kidney injury from an NSAID does not appear on the first panel. Creatinine rises over 24 to 72 hours, so a dog sent home Thursday night with normal values can be in established kidney failure by Sunday. If the dose landed in the renal range, the 48 to 72 hour recheck is not routine follow-up. It is the test that finds the damage.

A dog who vomits once and then eats dinner is not clear either. NSAID ulcers stay quiet for a day and then announce themselves as black stool or coffee-ground vomit. Both mean a clinic visit, not a wait.

The Visiting Dog Brings a Bottle Too

Your sister's Labrador is on carprofen, and his pills ride in the same bag as her Advil. A 75 mg chewable is the labeled daily dose for a dog around 37 lb. Eaten by a 10 lb terrier it is 16.5 mg/kg, close to four times the 4.4 mg/kg written on the box. Flavored chewables are worse, because that dog will eat one on purpose.

Two more guest-dog items take thirty seconds to fix. Feed the visiting dog and yours in separate closed rooms, since a holiday is a miserable week to discover resource guarding. And get the guest's food bag off the floor, because a 30 lb sack of kibble is roughly 45,000 calories and a dog will not stop at one bowl.

The other version of this call is quieter. Somebody notices the old dog is stiff on Thursday morning and offers half a tablet from their own bottle, meaning well. That is how a 10 lb dog reaches 22 mg/kg without a single bottle hitting the floor. Guests do not know that human pain relievers are not dog doses, and they will not ask.

Ten Minutes Before the Doorbell

  • Pick one room with a latch. Bags, coats, and boots go in it, and the door gets hooked, not just closed.
  • Move every pill organizer into a lidded case that sits on a shelf. A weekly box on a nightstand is seven doses at snout level.
  • Send one text the day before. Keep medications zipped, off the floor, and inside the guest room. Nobody minds being asked.
  • Tape the poison numbers to the fridge where a panicking person can read them without opening a phone.
  • Empty the bathroom trash before the first guest arrives. A discarded tablet works just as fast as a fresh one.

What a Thursday Ingestion Costs

The emergency exam runs $150 to $300 before anything is treated. Decontamination inside the window adds $100 to $250. An ulcer protocol with sucralfate, misoprostol, and fluids lands between $500 and $1,500. A dog needing 48 to 72 hours of IV fluids with serial bloodwork is looking at $1,500 to $3,500. Dialysis, which most practices cannot offer at all, starts near $8,000.

The number that matters is not the total. It is that a $12 bottle of pills left on a guest room floor can outrun the cost of the entire holiday.

Thanksgiving Medication Questions

How long after eating ibuprofen will a dog show signs?

Vomiting and drooling usually start within two to six hours. The damage that actually threatens the dog, meaning ulcers and kidney injury, takes a day or more to surface. Waiting for a symptom is the wrong strategy for exactly that reason.

Is one Advil dangerous for a 20 lb dog?

One 200 mg tablet on a 20 lb dog is 22 mg/kg, just under the 25 mg/kg line where most dogs show only mild stomach upset. It is not nothing and it is not a 3 a.m. emergency. Call poison control, watch for vomiting, and skip the home remedies.

Should I make my dog vomit if I catch him with the bottle?

Not on your own. Emesis helps only in the first hour or two, it risks aspiration, and it is the wrong move for a dog who is already drowsy or already vomiting. A veterinarian can induce it safely and follow with activated charcoal, which binds what is still sitting in the gut.

The Sweep, Then the Doorbell

Walk the guest room before anyone arrives, and look at floor level rather than counter level, because that is where a dog's nose works. Zip the bags yourself if you have to. Then put the numbers where you can find them in the dark.

Three red lines for the week. Any dog under 20 lb who ate any adult NSAID tablet gets a phone call now, not in the morning. Black or tarry stool, vomiting blood, or a dog who stops producing urine is an emergency room visit. A cat who licked or chewed acetaminophen goes in immediately, because 10 mg/kg is enough and one tablet is many times that.
