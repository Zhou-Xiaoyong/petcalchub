Boarding a Dog for Thanksgiving: The Real Cost of Four Nights

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

A kennel that quotes $45 a night is telling you four nights costs $180. The invoice that arrives in December is closer to $340, because the nightly rate is one line out of six and the other five get decided in the week you book.

Thanksgiving is the heaviest boarding weekend of the year in the United States. A facility that runs forty dogs has its holiday runs spoken for four to six weeks out, and the kennels still answering the phone on November 20 are often the ones with nobody on site overnight.

Quick answer: Four nights of Thanksgiving boarding typically lands between $320 and $560 once the holiday surcharge, a vaccine update, a trial day and a bath are added to a $35 to $55 nightly rate. Two drop-in visits a day for the same four days costs $144 to $224. Book this week, and write the ration in grams and calories rather than cups.

The Quote Is Not the Bill

The base rate is the part everyone compares and the smallest part of the total. A holiday surcharge of $5 to $15 a night is standard at facilities that publish rates, and kennels in a major metro run 30 to 50 percent above the national figure.

Two of those lines are worth a phone call. The vaccine line is not negotiable, because no facility takes a dog whose Bordetella is outside its window and the window is usually six to twelve months. The trial day is the one nobody asks about, and a dog that fails a temperament check on November 24 has no bed anywhere else in town.

Two Shots With Lead Times

Bordetella is the vaccine that runs out of time. The intranasal and oral forms build protection in about 72 hours, which is why most kennels will accept a dose given three days before check-in. The injectable form needs roughly two weeks, and it does less where the infection actually starts, in the nose and throat.

Canine influenza is the one owners forget. It takes two doses two to four weeks apart, and a dog with no history needs both, so a series started this week finishes around November 22 with days to spare. A series started the week of Thanksgiving cannot finish at all, and boarding is precisely the setting the vaccine is recommended for.

Then rabies and DHPP. If the rabies certificate expires in December, ask whether the facility will accept it for a stay that ends in November, because some will not. Carry paper or a photo of the certificate. A clinic asked to fax records on a holiday weekend is a clinic that is closed.

The Ration Has to Be Written in Grams

The intake form has a blank for food and amount, and most owners write "two cups, twice a day." Two cups of what, weighed how, decides whether a 40 lb dog on 862 calories, which is 1.4 times its resting energy, gets 862 or 1,120.

A level cup of large-breed kibble weighs about 90 grams. A cup of small-breed kibble weighs 115 to 130, because the pieces pack tighter. At 3.5 calories per gram that is 315 calories in the first cup and 455 in the second, and the kennel's own scoop is a third size again.

Write all four numbers. "123 grams, about 1.25 cups of this food, twice a day, 862 calories total," and tape the label from the bag to the container. Staff feeding thirty dogs read grams faster than cups, and the label settles any argument about whose measuring cup is on the counter.

Treats are the other half of the form. Three medium biscuits is around 120 calories, fourteen percent of an 862 calorie day, and a kennel hands out biscuits for good behavior all week. Either send your own and cap them at 85 calories, or write "no treats" and mean it.

Day One, the Bowl Comes Back Full

A dog in an unfamiliar run, with twenty other dogs barking, eats less. Twenty to forty percent less for the first day or two is ordinary and it is not a medical event. It turns into one when the kennel fixes it by pouring something out of a can on top, and the dog comes home with a new preference and four days of loose stool.

So put the instruction on the card: if he refuses two meals in a row, call me before adding anything to the bowl. Most facilities will honor that, and the ones that will not are worth knowing about before you hand over the leash.

Loose stool on day two or three is the most common call a kennel makes. Stress colitis settles with time and fiber, not with a diet change in the middle of a stay, and it is not a reason to drive four hours with a pot of rice.

The Pickup Check

Weigh the dog on your own scale at the time of day you normally do. One pound on a 40 lb dog is 2.5 percent, inside ordinary daily variation, so ignore it. Three pounds is a pattern.

Run a flat hand along the ribs and the spine before you start the car. A dog that ate 60 percent of its ration for four days will feel sharper there, and that is information about the next stay rather than a reason to double dinner.

Then watch the calendar. Kennel cough appears two to ten days after exposure, so a cough on December 2 belongs to Thanksgiving. Diarrhea that starts within 48 hours of pickup deserves a stool sample, because Giardia and the stress version look identical from the outside.

Count what came back in the bag, too. Half a bag of food returned means the dog ate half of what you sent, whatever the sheet at the front desk says.

Thanksgiving Boarding Questions

Is it cheaper to board or hire a sitter for four nights?

For one dog it is close. A kennel lands at $320 to $560 with the add-ons, while two drop-in visits a day for four days runs $144 to $224 with no holiday surcharge. With two dogs it flips, because a kennel charges per animal and most sitters add $5 to $15 for the second pet. The Pet Sitting Rate Calculator prices both in under a minute.

When should I book Thanksgiving boarding?

This week. Facilities fill four to six weeks ahead of the holiday, and the runs still open in the last ten days are usually at the places with no overnight attendant. A one-night deposit is normal and a 72-hour cancellation window is standard.

Will my dog lose weight at the kennel?

Often half a pound to two pounds over four nights, from reduced intake rather than illness, and it comes back within a week at home. Three pounds or more, or a dog that still will not eat 48 hours after pickup, is a veterinary visit.

Two numbers settle this holiday. The first is the invoice, and every line on it is knowable in the second week of November if you ask for it in writing. The second is the grams in the bowl, which takes the Dog Calorie Calculator and the label off your own bag.

The line at pickup: a cough that starts within ten days, blood in the stool, a dog refusing water for a full day, or one that has not eaten 48 hours after coming home. Those go to a vet. A thin dog and a sad first night are a note for next year.
