Poodle Calorie Calculator: Daily Food Needs

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published October 27, 2026 · 8 min read

The Poodle is three breeds wearing one name, and the food bowl is where that shows. A 5 lb Toy at 1.3× needs about 168 kcal a day. A 60 lb Standard at 1.6× needs 1,334. Same species, same bag of kibble, an eight-fold spread.

A house with both sizes has two unrelated feeding problems in the same kitchen. One dog's dinner is a third of a cup, the other's is closing on four, and neither number appears anywhere on the packaging.

Quick answer: Run RER = 70 × (kg)0.75, then multiply. A 5 lb (2.3 kg) Toy rests at 129 kcal; at 1.3 the target is 168. A 60 lb (27.2 kg) Standard rests at 834 kcal; at 1.6 the target is 1,334. The factor is where the sizes split: 1.3 for a companion Toy, 1.4 for a Miniature, 1.6 for a Standard that actually works. Put your own weight in the Dog Calorie Calculator.

Three Sizes, Three Body Weights

The AKC splits the breed by height first and weight second. Toys stand 10 inches or under and weigh 4 to 6 lb. Miniatures run 10 to 15 inches and 10 to 15 lb. Standards are over 15 inches and 40 to 70 lb, with males at the top of that range and females nearer 40 to 50.

Read those ranges twice and the hole appears. There is no size between 15 and 40 lb. A 25 lb Poodle is an oversized Miniature to the AKC and a small Medium to the FCI, which does recognize a Moyen at 35 to 45 cm and 11 to 19 kg. Every feeding chart printed in the US skips that dog entirely, and the owners of those dogs are the ones most likely to be guessing.

That last column is the part owners miss. A 5 lb Toy burns 34 kcal per pound of body weight. A 70 lb Standard burns 21. Small bodies run hotter per unit of mass, so a per-pound figure copied from a large dog overfeeds a Toy by roughly a third. Compare the sizes side by side in the Breed Calorie Calculator.

The Multiplier Splits With the Size

Published maintenance factors run from 1.2 for a neutered couch dog to 1.6 for one getting an hour of real exercise, with 1.8 reserved for working dogs in cold weather. Poodles cover the whole ladder, and which rung you pick is a size question, not a personality one.

The Standard was bred to retrieve waterfowl. That dog swam. An adult getting two 45-minute off-leash runs a day earns 1.6 without argument, and a field dog in November can hold 1.8. The Toy was bred to sit on a lap in a Paris apartment, and 1.3 is the honest figure for one that gets two 15-minute walks. The Miniature lands at 1.4.

Notice the asymmetry. Moving from 1.2 to 1.8 adds 118 kcal to the 6 lb Toy, about a third of a cup, and 626 to the 55 lb Standard, which is nearly two extra cups. Get the factor wrong on a Toy and you are off by a biscuit. Get it wrong on a Standard and you are off by a meal, every day, for years.

The Coat Hides the Scale

Body condition scoring wants eyes on a waist and hands on ribs. A Poodle in full coat defeats the first half of that. The curl sits over the loin exactly where the waist should dip, and a dog two pounds heavy reads as correct from across the room.

Two pounds is the whole game at the small end. On a 6 lb Toy that is 33 percent over ideal, which is a body condition score of 8 of 9, not a dog that could stand to lose a little. On a 60 lb Standard, two pounds is 3 percent and genuinely nothing. The same number on the same scale means opposite things depending on which Poodle is standing on it.

Use hands instead. Part the coat and lay a flat palm behind the shoulder. At ideal weight the ribs feel like the back of your hand, firm and easy to find under light pressure. Knuckles means too thin. The fleshy pad at the base of your thumb means there is a layer to remove. Then run a thumb down the spine and find both hip points without pressing hard. The Pet BMI & Ideal Weight Calculator turns that score into a target weight.

The groomer is not a scale, either. A Standard shaved down with a #7 blade loses the visual bulk of a full continental trim and looks four pounds lighter, and none of that was fat.

The 17-Calorie Treat Budget

The ten percent rule sounds generous until you run it on a Toy. Ten percent of 168 kcal is 17. A medium dog biscuit is about 40 kcal, so one biscuit is 2.4 times the entire daily allowance. A level tablespoon of peanut butter is 95, which is 5.6 times it.

On the 60 lb Standard, ten percent of 1,334 is 133 kcal. A biscuit disappears inside that, which is how one household can treat two dogs identically and wreck only one of them.

The fix is not a healthier treat. Pull the treat calories out of the measured ration instead of stacking them on top, and hand over single kibbles from the cup you already weighed. A Toy Poodle that gets four pieces of its own dinner is exactly as pleased as one that gets a biscuit, and the math stays closed.

Two Problems That Look Like Math Errors

Standard Poodles are the breed most often reported with hypoadrenocorticism, Addison's disease. The classic form produces weight loss, poor appetite, vomiting and weakness, so the dog looks like a ration that should hold weight is refusing to hold it. No scoop size fixes that.

The trap is the atypical form. Electrolytes stay normal, so a normal sodium-to-potassium ratio does not rule Addison's out. The ACTH stimulation test does. A Standard losing weight while eating well needs that test before anyone touches the food.

The second is bloat. The Standard is deep-chested, which puts it on the GDV list beside Danes, Shepherds and Setters. Two meals beat one, a raised bowl raises risk rather than lowering it, and a dog that retches without bringing anything up is an emergency, not a feeding question. At 1,334 kcal, two meals is 667 each.

At the small end the risk runs the other way. Toys carry patellar luxation and can run low on blood sugar when meals are spaced too far apart, so a weight-loss plan for a 4 lb dog moves in 10 percent steps and never in 30.

What to Change This Week

Weigh one cup of your kibble in grams. Small-breed formulas are denser per cup, often 400 to 480 kcal against 330 to 380 for large-breed foods. At 480 kcal a cup, the Toy's 168 kcal target is 0.35 cup, a volume nobody scoops accurately twice in a row.

Dry kibble carries only about 10 percent moisture, so the water bowl matters as much as the food bowl. The Dog Water Intake Calculator gives the daily target by body weight, and a Poodle that stops drinking is a vet call, not a portion tweak.

Weigh the dog on the same scale every two weeks, and cut 10 percent at a time with three weeks between changes. A 1 lb error on a 5 lb dog is 20 percent of the animal, so a bathroom scale will not do.

Three things stop being calculator questions. A Poodle that vomits after most meals. A Standard that stays thin on a ration that should hold it. A Toy that goes weak or wobbly between meals. Those are exams. Everything else is a number in a bowl you can change tonight.

Pick the size first. Nothing downstream of that choice is worth calculating until the dog is measured against the right range.
