Dog Got Into the Thanksgiving Trash? The 72-Hour Pancreatitis Window

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 21, 2026 · 8 min read

At 11:40 on Thanksgiving night the kitchen bag is empty, the dog is asleep on the rug, and the house has moved on to pie. Nothing about the next twelve hours looks like an emergency. That is the trouble with garbage.

Count what a modest raid holds. A strip of turkey skin, two spoons of pan drippings, half a cup of stuffing, the butter dish with a tablespoon still in it. That is roughly 780 calories and 59 grams of fat. For a 25 lb dog it is more than a full day of food, eaten in four minutes.

Quick answer: fat has no mg/kg threshold, so this is decided on signs rather than arithmetic. Vomiting that starts 12 to 72 hours after the raid, usually on day two, a dog who refuses food, and an arched back with the belly tucked in are reasons to call. If it has been under two hours and the bag held bones, foil, plastic wrap, or twine, call now instead of waiting for a symptom. What Was Actually in the Bag

A trash bag rarely contains one thing. It contains the parts of the meal nobody put on a plate, and the fat in those parts is concentrated by cooking.

Portions are by eye and composition follows the USDA food database. Daily totals use resting energy requirement times 1.6, the formula behind the calculator: about 690 calories at 25 lb and 1,500 at 70 lb. A bigger dog does not get proportionally more slack, because a bigger bag gets eaten too.

Why There Is No mg/kg Line for Fat

Chocolate has a number. Theobromine at 20 mg/kg starts the stomach upset, 40 to 50 mg/kg drives the heart rate up, and 60 mg/kg is the seizure range. Xylitol has a number as well: 100 mg/kg drops blood glucose. Fat does not have one, and no study has produced a grams-per-kilogram threshold for pancreatitis in dogs.

What the veterinary literature supports is a relationship rather than a cutoff. High-fat meals, garbage raids, and sudden table-scrap binges are the triggers reported most often in canine acute pancreatitis, and the risk tracks the size of the load against how well that particular dog handles fat. A dog with fasting triglycerides around or above 500 mg/dL is a different patient from a lean dog with normal lipids, even at the same body weight.

This is why the arithmetic on this page is a starting point, not a verdict. A nine-year-old Labrador who has been eating scraps off the floor since he was a puppy may raid the bag and show nothing. A Miniature Schnauzer with familial hypertriglyceridemia may get sick from a fraction of the same load.

The 24-to-72 Hour Clock

Pancreatitis does not announce itself at midnight. The gland inflames over hours, and the signs arrive a day or two later, which is exactly when the family has stopped paying attention.

The first 24 hours decide whether you call. The 48 after that decide how bad it gets.

Clock What is happening What to do
0 to 2 hoursFood is still in the stomach. Bones, foil, and plastic wrap are the immediate hazards, not the fatCall the vet or poison control. Induction is a clinic decision this early, never a kitchen one
2 to 12 hoursFat is being absorbed. The pancreas has not complained yetMost dogs look completely normal. Do not read that as an all-clear
12 to 24 hoursFirst vomiting, food refusal, a back arched and a belly tuckedSame-day exam. This is the window most owners actually call in
24 to 48 hoursPeak presentation for most casesBloodwork, cPL, ultrasound if the picture is muddy
48 to 72 hoursStill inside the windowA dog eating normally at hour 72 has cleared the pancreatitis risk. Bones run on a separate schedule

The pancreas-specific lipase test, cPL, is the best single blood marker for this. It is also not perfect: sensitivity in the first day is good but not total, which is why a normal result at hour 20 in a dog who is still vomiting buys a recheck rather than a discharge.

The First Two Hours: What Helps and What Hurts

  • Call before you clean up. A photo of the empty bag and the packaging is worth more to the vet than a guess. Do not throw the evidence out.
  • Do not make him vomit at home. Hydrogen peroxide causes stomach ulceration, the dose is easy to get wrong, and it is the wrong move entirely if the bag held bones, foil, or plastic wrap on the way back up.
  • Look under the tongue. Truss twine and plastic wrap can catch there and act as a linear foreign body. If you see string, do not pull it. Call.
  • Leave the water down. Small sips are fine. Withholding water from a dog who may be about to vomit for two days is the wrong direction.
  • Skip the bread and rice. Soaking up the fat is not a thing. Adding a starch bomb to a pancreas that is already unhappy helps nobody.
  • Check for mold. Moldy bread or dairy in the bag carries tremorgenic mycotoxins, and those cause fine muscle tremors, high body temperature, and seizures within 30 minutes to 3 hours. That is a poison control call, not a wait-and-see.

Who Gets Sick From This

Breed matters more than most owners expect. Miniature Schnauzers lead the list because familial hypertriglyceridemia runs in the breed. Yorkshire Terriers, Cocker Spaniels, and Cavalier King Charles Spaniels are over-represented in case series as well. So are overweight dogs, dogs over about eight, and any dog who has had one episode already, since a previous episode is the strongest predictor of the next one.

Body condition is the part you can change this week. A dog at a body condition score of 7 out of 9 is carrying a risk factor that shows up in the literature repeatedly, and the Pet BMI and Ideal Weight Calculator puts a number on it. What Friday Morning Costs

An exam runs $150 to $300 at a daytime practice, more at an emergency hospital on a holiday weekend. Bloodwork with a cPL adds $200 to $400. Twenty-four to forty-eight hours of IV fluids, anti-nausea medication, and pain control usually lands between $1,000 and $2,500. A severe case that needs three to five days in intensive care runs $3,000 to $8,000, and the dogs who do worst are the ones who arrived on day three instead of day one.

Pet insurance will not rescue this particular weekend. Most policies exclude new conditions for 14 days after the policy starts, so a policy bought on Thursday covers nothing on Friday.

Thanksgiving Trash Questions

How long after eating garbage do pancreatitis signs show up?

Typically 12 to 72 hours, with most cases presenting between 24 and 48. A dog who is fine at bedtime on Thursday and vomiting on Friday evening fits the pattern exactly. The false all-clear at hour 8 is the most common reason people wait.

Should I make my dog throw up after a trash raid?

Not on your own. Induction only makes sense inside roughly two hours, only if the load was non-caustic, and only when a vet has looked at what went in. Bones and foil turn vomiting into a second injury, and peroxide has its own toxicity.

Can one fatty meal cause pancreatitis?

Yes, and a first episode is often exactly that in a dog with no history. It is more likely in a dog who is already overweight, already has high triglycerides, or has had an episode before, which is why two dogs can eat the same bag and only one ends up on fluids.

Take the Bag Out Before Bed

The cheapest intervention on this page happens at 10 p.m. on Thursday. Tie the bag, walk it to the bin outside, and put the lid down. A locking can is the second version of the same idea. Everything else on this page is what you do after that failed.

The red lines are repeated vomiting, a dog who will not eat for 24 hours, a stance with the front legs down and the rear end up, or gums that look pale. Those mean the clinic now, holiday or not. If you are past 72 hours and he is eating and holding it down, the pancreatitis window has closed. The bone and foreign-body clock is a separate one, and it does not stop just because the vomiting did.
