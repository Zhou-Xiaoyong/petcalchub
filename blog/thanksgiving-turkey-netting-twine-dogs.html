Dog Ate Turkey Netting or Twine? The 12-Hour Rule for a Linear Obstruction

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 25, 2026 · 8 min read

The netting comes off the breast in one piece and still holds the shape of the bird. It goes in the sink because the trash bag is across the kitchen and the oven door is open. You turn to baste. When you turn back, the sink is empty and the dog is licking its muzzle.

Three things leave a turkey that are not food: the nylon netting, the trussing twine, and the pop-up thermometer. Owners worry about all three the way they worry about a bone. None of them fails like a bone.

Quick answer: A string or mesh with one end caught is a linear foreign body. Peristalsis pulls the free length tight and the intestine pleats around it, which can kill or tear the wall in 12 to 24 hours. Do not pull it. Do not cut it and hope. Twine at the gum line, or a dog that cannot hold water down, is a tonight case.

Why a String Is Not a Bone

A bone is a rigid object in a flexible tube. The question is whether it fits, and the answer arrives at the first narrow point. A string does not need to fit. It anchors.

Once one end is fixed, every peristaltic wave drags the free length tighter, and the bowel folds forward along that line every few centimeters. Vets call it a plicated bowel. The folds pinch their own blood supply, and the wall being sawed against a taut cord is the wall that gives way.

Which is why six inches of cotton twine can be worse than three feet of it. Length is not the dose. The anchor is.

The Anchor Point Decides Everything

Twine catches in four places: under the tongue at the frenulum, around a lower molar, at the pylorus, and occasionally where the small intestine meets the large.

The first two you can check in four seconds. Lift the lip, open the mouth, look under the tongue and along both gum lines. Twine across the tongue is anchored, and that dog needs a clinic, not scissors.

Nylon netting behaves differently. Heat-shrunk around the breast, it dries into a hard knot rather than loose mesh. The mesh usually travels. The knot stops at the pylorus.

The 12-Hour Clock

Hour six is the trap. A dog with a plicated intestine often looks normal, eats a little, and sleeps, while the damage happens in a bowel that is not sending pain signals.

Hour 0 to 6. Nothing, or one vomit and a dog that seems fine. Drooling and lip licking matter more than they look, especially with netting.

Hour 6 to 12. Vomiting becomes the pattern. Small amounts, then foam, then water coming back within minutes. A dog that vomits water is obstructed.

Hour 12 to 24. The belly turns tender and the dog stands hunched. No stool, or a few mucous stools. The wall can perforate anywhere in this window, without a sign you would recognize at home.

Hour 24 to 48. Peritonitis: fever of 103 to 105 degrees Fahrenheit, a belly rigid to a light touch, vomiting that will not stop, a dog that stops answering to its name. Surgery tonight, with a worse prognosis than the same dog at hour eight.

The clock runs shorter than the bone clock because a bone waits to travel. A string does damage where it lands.

What Not to Do in the First Hour

Do not pull, and do not cut it at the mouth and assume the rest passes. If the far end is anchored, traction tears the anchor loose or saws the cord through the intestinal wall. Cutting leaves the anchored stump where it was.

Do not reach for hydrogen peroxide. Vomiting a taut string back up an esophagus does not dislodge it, and repeated doses cause hemorrhagic gastritis.

Do not feed bread, pumpkin, or rice to cushion it. Bulk does not wrap a cord. It packs behind the fold and closes a partial blockage.

What to do instead: check the mouth once, photograph the packaging so you can name the material, call the clinic, offer small amounts of water. Go in if the dog cannot hold water down, if twine is visible in the mouth, or if it has vomited more than twice.

What the Veterinarian Will Do

Expect an exam, then imaging. Ultrasound is the useful test: a plicated bowel reads as a stack of parallel folds with the cord as a bright line through the middle. Radiographs show gas patterns and catch a metal spring.

Surgery is not one incision. Each plicated loop gets its own enterotomy so the surgeon can lift the string out in sections, and dead wall gets resected. The string is never pulled from the mouth, because the pull caused the damage.

Dogs operated on before perforation do well. Dogs operated on after peritonitis spend days in intensive care. The gap between those outcomes is measured in hours.

Feeding After the Incision Closes

Most dogs go home on day one or two with vague-sounding instructions. Four small meals a day for 48 hours, water free choice once the dog is awake.

The target is not the maintenance number. Tissue repair runs about 20 percent above resting energy requirement, so the Dog Calorie Calculator gives you maintenance, and the recovery meal is 1.2 times the resting number, not the full maintenance number.

Skip the Thanksgiving plate for a week. Fat is the last thing a recovering gut needs.

Thanksgiving Questions

Can I pull the string if I can see it?

No. String at the mouth or under the tongue means one end is anchored, which makes traction the most damaging thing you can do. String at the anus means the same from the other direction. Cover it, stop the dog sitting on it, and drive.

Will the netting pass on its own?

Sometimes. Loose mesh often does, and a dog eating, drinking, and passing stool 24 hours later has probably cleared it. The dried knot at the center of a breast sock is the part that does not, and nothing at home tells you which one your dog got.

The dog is fine at hour eight. Is that good news?

It is not evidence of anything. Plication does not cause dramatic pain early, and the classic error is a normal-looking dog at bedtime and a perforation by breakfast. Water retention and stool are the markers that count.

The Bottom Line

Check the mouth in the first sixty seconds and pull nothing you find there. Call the clinic with the material in hand, because netting, twine, and a thermometer behave differently. A dog holding water down at hour eight can be watched. A dog vomiting water, drooling with twine at the gum line, or standing hunched with a hard belly goes in now. Waiting is the difference between a $2,500 enterotomy and a week in intensive care.
