Can Cats Eat Thanksgiving Turkey? A 260-Calorie Answer

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

A 10 lb cat needs about 260 calories for the entire day. One ounce of roasted turkey skin is 125 of them, and a quarter cup of gravy poured over the bird is another 90.

Cats also skip the floor. They get on the counter, and the turkey rests up there under loose foil for half an hour before anyone carves.

Quick answer: Plain roasted turkey is safe in a piece about the size of a walnut, roughly 40 calories. Skin, gravy, stuffing and anything seasoned are not. A 10 lb cat's whole day is 260 calories, so the table is a small slice of it. The two emergencies specific to cats this week are a string off the trussed bird and, in an overweight cat, three days of not eating.

The Day Is 260 Calories, Not a Share of Yours

Resting energy for a 10 lb cat is 70 times 4.54 kilograms raised to the 0.75 power, which comes to 218 calories. An indoor neutered adult runs about 1.2 times that, so 260. The Cat Calorie Calculator returns the same number from your cat's weight and body condition, and it is worth writing down before Thursday.

An ounce of dark meat with the skin on is a fifth of the day. An ounce of skin alone is nearly half, and nobody measures skin, because it peels off the bird in strips. A cat that licks one strip and walks away has eaten a meal and a half.

The Counter Is the Cat's Floor

Thanksgiving warnings are written for dogs because a dog's nose lives at floor level. A healthy adult cat clears a 36 inch counter without a pause, and a 14 lb bird on the stove sits at nose height for a cat on a chair back.

That resting window is the biggest exposure of the day. The bird comes out of a 350 degree oven, sits for 30 to 60 minutes, and the kitchen empties. Foil peeled back at a corner is an invitation. Put the pan in the cold oven, or tent it and stay in the room.

The sink runs second. Giblets in a colander, the neck left in the cavity, the pan soaking with an inch of fat. Raw poultry carries Salmonella and Campylobacter, and a cat that laps the sink can be colonized and shed both.

Then the front door. Guests arrive in a group with their hands full, and an indoor-only cat is the fastest thing through the gap. Close the cat in a back room before the first car pulls in.

Turkey Is Fine. Twine Is Not.

A walnut-sized piece of plain roasted turkey is about 8 grams of protein, close to half of what a 10 lb cat needs in a day, for 40 calories. No salt, no rub, no butter, no drippings.

The bird is trussed with butcher's twine, sometimes netting, sometimes a pop-up thermometer. A cat's tongue is covered in papillae that point backward, so a cat cannot work a strand back out once it is in the mouth. The string fixes under the tongue or at the pylorus, the bowel gathers into an accordion behind the anchor, and that is a linear foreign body. It is a surgery.

A string you can see at the mouth or under the tail is a same-day visit, and you do not pull it. Expect $1,500 to $3,000 if the bowel is still viable.

The Sweet Myth and the Poison That Is Real

Cats cannot taste sweetness. The sweet receptor gene Tas1r2 is a pseudogene in cats, shut off somewhere in their evolution as obligate carnivores. A cat does not want your pumpkin pie. That is also why chocolate calls to poison control are overwhelmingly about dogs.

The xylitol panic is a dog problem too. It drops a dog's blood sugar by triggering insulin release, and there is no matching feline case series. Gum on the floor is still a foreign body risk for a cat, just not a hypoglycemia one.

Onion and garlic run the other way. Cats are more sensitive than dogs, because feline hemoglobin oxidizes more readily and Heinz body anemia arrives on a smaller dose. Onion powder is the concentrated form, about eight times the sulfur compounds of the fresh vegetable, and a teaspoon weighs 2.4 grams. Against the dog threshold of 5 grams per kilogram of fresh onion, a 10 lb cat is at the line on one teaspoon. Assume the cat is worse, because it is.

Do Not Put a Cat on a Holiday Diet

An overweight cat that stops eating for two to three days can develop hepatic lipidosis. Fat mobilizes to the liver faster than the liver can package and export it, the liver yellows, and the treatment is a feeding tube for weeks. The cure is calories, and the whole thing starts with a well-meant decision to cut the cat back after the holidays.

If your cat does need to lose weight, weigh the food in grams, use the cat calorie calculator for the target, and aim for half a pound a month. The Pet BMI calculator gives the ideal weight, and cat water intake is the number to watch on dry food.

Thanksgiving Questions From Cat Owners

Can my cat have a piece of the turkey?

Yes, plain meat with no skin, rub, butter or gravy, in a piece the size of a walnut. That is 40 calories, 15 percent of a 10 lb cat's day. Cut it into two or three pieces, because a cat that bolts one chunk brings it back up on the rug.

My cat licked gravy that had onion powder in it. How much is too much?

No feline threshold is published, so work from the dog numbers and assume less. One teaspoon is 2.4 grams, the 5 g/kg line for a 10 lb animal before any feline correction. Call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 with weight, amount and time, then watch the gums and the urine for five days.

Is it safe to skip my cat's dinner because of the big meal?

No. One skipped meal is fine for a lean cat and a bad idea for a heavy one. The lipidosis window opens around 48 to 72 hours of anorexia, and a Thursday of guests plus a quiet Friday is long enough to get there.
