Sugar-Free Thanksgiving Pie and Dogs: The 100 mg/kg Xylitol Line

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 17, 2026 · 8 min read

One piece of sugar-free gum is about 300 milligrams of xylitol. A pack listing xylitol first in the ingredients runs past 1,000. A 9 lb Yorkie is 4.1 kilograms, so a single piece is 73 to 244 mg/kg: a phone call at the bottom, a dextrose drip at the top.

Thanksgiving puts three of those packs in the house at once. The purse by the door, the candy dish for the kids, and the pie a guest brought because she counts carbs. The bird is not the problem this year.

Quick answer: Xylitol is the only thing on a Thanksgiving dessert table that can kill a dog before Friday. Hypoglycemia starts near 0.1 g/kg of body weight, liver injury at 0.5 g/kg, and a pie baked with a quarter cup of xylitol is about 5,900 mg a slice. Run his weight through our xylitol toxicity calculator before the doorbell.

Xylitol Is the Only Thing on That Table That Can Kill by Friday

Xylitol absorbs slowly in people and barely moves insulin. In dogs it peaks in the blood at about 30 minutes and drives an insulin release 2.7 to 7 times larger than the same weight of glucose. The brain runs on glucose, so the first signs are a brain running out: wobbling, weakness, collapse, tremors, seizures.

The lines vets work from are the ASPCA's: hypoglycemia likely at 0.1 g/kg, liver injury at 0.5 g/kg. The low end is not a guarantee. A published case series reported hypoglycemia at 0.03 g/kg, which is why our calculator flags at 50 mg/kg.

Signs usually land 30 to 60 minutes in, and can hold off for 12 hours. A dog who looks normal at four in the afternoon has not been cleared. Liver enzymes and clotting times are the second act, 12 to 48 hours out, and that is the part that kills.

Activated charcoal binds xylitol poorly, so the standard decontamination move is close to useless here. Emesis helps only in a dog with no signs yet, inside the first half hour.

One Cup of Xylitol Weighs 190 Grams

Bakers do not measure like gum manufacturers. The APCC's working figure for powdered xylitol is a cup at about 190 g, which is 190,000 mg. Sixteen tablespoons to the cup, so a level tablespoon is 11.9 g.

Do the pan in your kitchen. A quarter cup in the filling, cut into eight slices, is 5,900 mg a slice. A 20 lb beagle is 9.1 kg, so one slice is 648 mg/kg, past the liver line before anyone offers seconds. The same slice for a 70 lb Lab is 186 mg/kg, above the hypoglycemia line and under the liver one, which still means an emergency visit.

The label does not always say xylitol. Look for birch sugar, birch bark extract, or a vague "natural sweetener." Sugar-free, no sugar added and diabetic-friendly exclude nothing, and a pie with no added sugar can be sweetened entirely with it.

The Three Desserts That Are Not Xylitol

Nutmeg is the second. Myristicin causes tremors, agitation and, at a high enough dose, seizures, usually 6 to 24 hours after a dog licks out the bowl. Fatal cases are rare and there is no tidy mg/kg threshold, which is why the grater should not sit out overnight.

Mincemeat is the quiet one. Raisins behave like grapes: acute kidney injury, no established safe dose, and an idiosyncratic response, so one dog eats them for years and another fails from a handful.

Rum cake and bourbon pecan pie carry ethanol, with a published lethal dose in dogs of 5.5 to 7.9 g/kg and signs well below it. A 10 lb dog who laps two tablespoons of rum sauce takes in roughly 3 to 4 g, or 0.7 to 0.9 g/kg: vomiting, wobbling, a long night.

Then plain fat. A slice of pecan pie is 500 to 600 kcal and 25 to 30 g of fat, against about 700 kcal for a 20 lb dog's whole day on the dog calorie calculator. A dog with a pancreatitis history has a 48-hour fuse on a meal like that. The full accounting is in how much Thanksgiving food a dog can eat.

The First Thirty Minutes

Do not wait for a symptom. The peak is at 30 minutes and the insulin surge follows it, so a dog who looks fine at minute 20 is the ordinary case, not the reassuring one. Call the clinic or ASPCA APCC at (888) 426-4435 while he is still standing.

If signs have started, rub honey or corn syrup on the gums on the way to the car. Glucose crosses gum tissue and buys minutes. Do not pour syrup down the throat of a dog who is seizing, do not induce vomiting unless a vet tells you to, and skip the charcoal. Bring the package with you.

Expect 12 to 24 hours of glucose monitoring for a dose in the hypoglycemia band, and 72 hours of liver and clotting checks past 0.5 g/kg. Treatment runs a 1 to 2 mL/kg bolus of 25% dextrose, then fluids with 2.5% to 5% dextrose, plus SAMe at 17 to 20 mg/kg a day. That is the bill a $9 pack of gum is trying to hand you.

The Aunt Who Brings a Sugar-Free Pie

This is where most xylitol cases begin, and it is not carelessness. A guest managing her own blood sugar brings a dessert she can eat, sets it on the coffee table, and cuts the dog a slice because pumpkin is supposed to be good for him.

The fix is one sentence at the door, not at the table: the dog cannot have anything with xylitol or birch sugar, because it drops his blood sugar inside an hour. Most people have never heard it.

Then move the objects. Guest bags go in a closed room instead of the coat pile. The candy dish goes above counter height. The pie stays in the kitchen with a label on the foil, and the dog gets two tablespoons of plain canned pumpkin at the same moment everyone else gets dessert, because a dog with a bowl is a dog who is not under the table. The guest side of this is in Thanksgiving guests and your dog.

Thanksgiving Dessert Questions

Is one bite of sugar-free pie dangerous?

Depends on the weight and on a recipe nobody at the table knows. A slice of the quarter-cup pie is 1,439 mg/kg for a 9 lb dog, nearly three times the liver line, and a bite is not a small share of a slice. For a 70 lb dog it is probably under 50 mg/kg. Use the calculator instead of estimating.

He seems fine an hour later. Can I wait?

No. A dog with no signs at one hour is the normal presentation, and hypoglycemia can also arrive at hour 12. Glucose is cheap to check early and expensive to fix at eleven at night on a holiday. Call with the milligrams and the weight.

Is plain pumpkin pie bad for dogs on its own?

The pumpkin is not the problem. Canned plain pumpkin is fiber, and a tablespoon is a fine treat. Filling adds sugar, evaporated milk, eggs and nutmeg, the crust adds butter, and a slice lands near 320 kcal and 14 g of fat. Sensitive gut, diarrhea. Pancreatitis history, something worse.

A dog's Thanksgiving does not need a dessert. Two tablespoons of plain pumpkin, white meat off the bone, and his own bowl when everyone else gets theirs is the whole allowance.

Go to the emergency hospital now if he is wobbling, cannot stand, has tremors or a seizure, or if the math lands above 0.5 g/kg on a dog under 30 lb. ASPCA APCC is at (888) 426-4435 and Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661, both with a consultation fee.
