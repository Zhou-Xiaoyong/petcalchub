German Shepherd Calorie Calculator: Daily Food Needs

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published October 24, 2026 · 8 min read

A 75 lb German Shepherd walked an hour a day needs about 1,580 kcal. The same dog in IGP training three nights a week needs 1,972. Nothing changed but the odometer, and no bag of food prints both numbers.

That gap is where most feeding mistakes happen. The resting formula is fixed. The multiplier is not, and this is one of the few breeds where the honest multiplier runs up.

Quick answer: Run RER = 70 × (kg)0.75, then multiply. A neutered adult with an hour of real exercise gets 1.6, a couch dog 1.2, a sport or patrol dog 2.0 to 2.5, and a weight-loss plan starts at 1.0 of the target weight. A 75 lb (34 kg) dog sits at 986 kcal at rest and 1,578 at 1.6×. Enter your own dog's numbers in the Dog Calorie Calculator.

A Breed That Spans 35 Pounds

The AKC standard puts males at 65 to 90 lb and females at 55 to 75. The German SV standard is wider still: 30 to 40 kg (66 to 88 lb) for males, 22 to 32 kg (49 to 71 lb) for females. That is 35 lb of legitimate range inside one breed, and 35 lb is worth roughly 350 kcal a day.

Bloodlines matter more than the standard does. American show lines are heavy-boned, and a finished male at 88 lb is not fat, he is bred that way. East German and Czech working lines are leaner, often 60 to 75 lb, and those are frequently the dogs doing bitework four nights a week. Feeding a working-line dog off a show-line chart is a 300 kcal error every day.

The Arithmetic on a 75 lb Dog

75 lb is 34.0 kg. Raise it to the 0.75 power and you get 12.6. Multiply by 70 and resting energy requirement is 986 kcal. At 1.6 for a neutered adult with an hour of daily exercise, the daily target is 1,578 kcal.

Most large-breed kibble runs 330 to 380 kcal per cup. At 360, that is 4.4 cups a day, or 2.2 cups per meal. An owner who free-pours into a coffee mug lands near 6 cups, which is 2,160 kcal. That is a 580 kcal surplus, close to a pound of fat every six days.

Where the Multiplier Actually Sits

The 1.6 factor was written for a dog getting an hour of genuine exercise. Most German Shepherds get 40 minutes of sniffing at a pace that covers a mile. Good for the dog, poor for calorie burn, and both get logged as an hour.

This is also the breed where the factor can climb. An IGP dog trains three to four nights a week and carries 2.0 to 2.4. A patrol K9 sits at 2.5 and up. No leash walk reaches those rows.

When Weight Loss Is Not a Math Problem

German Shepherds are overrepresented in exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, where the pancreas stops making enough digestive enzymes. In several US case series the breed accounts for roughly 70 percent of cases.

The dog eats ravenously, may steal food or eat stool, and still loses weight. Stool turns pale, bulky, greasy, and more frequent. The coat gets dull. The dog looks like it is starving while eating twice what it should.

Diagnosis is a fasting trypsin-like immunoreactivity (TLI) test. A result below 2.5 µg/L confirms it. Treatment is powdered enzymes mixed into every meal plus cobalamin for the many dogs also low in B12, and most regain weight within weeks.

This is where owners usually go wrong. A dog that looks thin gets a bigger scoop, and 2,000 kcal goes into a gut that cannot absorb it. Weight loss with a good appetite is a blood test, not a larger portion.

One Deep Chest, One Big Meal

The breed sits in the high-risk group for gastric dilatation-volvulus. Lifetime risk across large breeds runs near 5.7 percent in the Glickman studies, and higher for German Shepherds and their first-degree relatives.

Two findings surprise owners. A raised bowl raised the risk rather than lowering it, and so did one large meal a day. A full stomach has more gas to trap, and a stretched stomach rotates on its short attachments.

Feed two or three smaller meals, put the bowl on the floor, and slow down a dog that finishes in 90 seconds. Unproductive retching, a tight distended belly, pacing, and drooling are not wait-and-see signs. That dog goes to a clinic now, because GDV kills within hours.

Hips, Elbows, and the One Lever You Own

German Shepherds carry some of the highest rates of hip and elbow dysplasia of any breed. The trait is polygenic, so a clear sire and dam still produce affected puppies, and no ration changes that.

Body condition is the part you control. The same dysplastic hip carries more load at 6 of 9 than at 5 of 9, and that shows up as a shorter comfortable walk long before a limp.

Degenerative myelopathy belongs here for one reason: weight. The SOD1:c.118G>A mutation is common in the breed and causes a slow, painless rear-leg weakness with knuckling and crossed paws. A lean dog with DM stays mobile longer. Keep the ribs findable and check a target weight in the Pet BMI & Ideal Weight Calculator.

Eight Weeks to Two Years

This breed grows for 18 to 24 months. Large-breed growth diets cap calcium at 1.0 to 1.8 percent on a dry-matter basis for that reason. Extra calcium in a fast-growing giant is an orthopedic problem, not a bonus.

Panosteitis shows up between 5 and 12 months as a lameness that shifts from one leg to another over days. It hurts and it resolves on its own, and it is not a reason to cut the ration. Growing puppies need the calories.

What to Change This Week

Weigh one cup of kibble on a kitchen scale and write the grams on the bin lid. Two cups by volume can be 40 g off between brands, and on a 1,578 kcal ration that drift is a meal a week. Then split the day's 4.4 cups across two bowls.

Treats get 10 percent, about 155 kcal here. A large dental chew is 60 to 100 kcal, so two of them eat most of that budget. On training days they come out of the kibble ration, not on top of it.

Weigh the dog every two weeks, same scale, same time of day. A bathroom scale is good to about half a pound, which is fine for a trend. Change the portion 10 percent at a time and give each change three weeks.

Three things stop being calculator questions. A dog that vomits after most meals. A dog losing weight on a ration that should hold it. A dog that cannot finish a walk it managed last month. Those are exams. Everything else is a number in a bowl you can change tonight.
