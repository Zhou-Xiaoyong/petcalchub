How Much to Feed a Chihuahua: Portions by Weight and Age

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

A four-pound Chihuahua eats about 36 grams of kibble a day. In the units that actually exist in a kitchen, that is four and a half level tablespoons, split into two meals of 18 grams each.

Most measuring cups begin at a quarter cup. Fill one with small-breed kibble and it weighs 26 to 30 grams, which is already 70 to 80 percent of this dog's day before the scoop is leveled.

The breed standard allows up to six pounds, and plenty of pets live between two and four. At that size the portion is not a scoop question but a spoon question, and the only instrument that answers it honestly is a scale that reads single grams.

Where the grams come from: resting energy is 70 times body weight in kilograms raised to the 0.75 power. A 4 lb (1.8 kg) Chihuahua rests at 109 kcal; at 1.4 for a dog with two short walks that becomes 153 kcal. Divide by the food's kilocalories per gram and the bowl holds 36 grams. A Ration Measured in Tablespoons

Dry food carries between 3.2 and 4.5 kilocalories per gram, and the label states it as kcal/kg. A 4,200 kcal/kg formula is 4.2 kcal per gram. A 3,200 kcal/kg formula, which is where most weight-control and senior foods sit, is 3.2. Same 153 calories, and the bowl weight moves from 36 grams to 48. A third more food for an identical day.

Kibble geometry adds a second error on top. A level tablespoon of the small, dense kibble sold for toy breeds weighs about 8 grams; a flat, wide adult kibble in the same spoon settles nearer 6.

The scale is what makes the table usable. Bowl on, press tare, spoon until the display reaches the number. Eleven grams is eleven or twelve pieces of small-breed kibble, and no two of those pieces weigh the same, which is why the count is not the unit.

Eight Weeks to Twelve Months

A Chihuahua puppy arrives at six to eight weeks weighing somewhere under two pounds, often closer to one and a half. Birth weight in the breed runs under four ounces, and the whole first year is a climb from that to five or six pounds.

One working estimate survives the trip to the vet's office: double the weight at eight weeks, then add a little. A puppy at 1.75 lb at eight weeks tends to land near six, which is why a two-pound eight-week-old is unremarkable.

Growth energy sits on a four-rung ladder above resting requirement. Under four months, 3.0 times resting energy; four to six months, 2.5; six to nine months, 2.2; the last stretch to maturity, 1.8. The grams that come out are not intuitive, and the meal count changes underneath them.

Two rows in that table catch owners out. Three months and four months both come to 55 grams, because the puppy gains a third of a pound while the factor drops a fifth; the two effects cancel. Then between five and six months the total falls from 65 grams to 63 while the puppy keeps growing. A dog that leaves food in the bowl at six months is often just being overfed by a chart nobody revised.

Months four through seven: the adult teeth come through and the mouth hurts. Intake commonly falls by a fifth for a week or two, and owners respond by leaving the bowl down all day or switching to wet food. Soften the kibble with warm water for ten minutes instead, keep the same weighed ration, and give it back at the next meal. A puppy that refuses food for more than a day at this age needs the mouth looked at, not a different flavor.

Five Meals, Then Four, Then Three, Then Two

Meal count follows stomach capacity, not preference. At eight weeks a Chihuahua puppy is on four or five small meals, and the last one sits late enough that the overnight gap stays near six hours. Under three pounds there is very little liver glycogen to draw on between meals, which is why the breed's hypoglycemia reputation exists and why the spacing matters more than the total.

By ten or twelve weeks most puppies handle four meals, three carries from about four months to six, and two from six months onward. Adult Chihuahuas do best on two. One large meal a day in a dog this small means a long empty stretch, and the sign is the morning vomit of yellow foam that owners describe as a sensitive stomach. Moving dinner two hours later fixes more of those cases than any food change. For the calorie target itself and the breed's six-pound show-ring ceiling, the companion piece on the Chihuahua calorie target works through that side of the arithmetic.

The Bowl That Is Never Empty

Free-feeding is the default in this breed, and it is the one habit that makes every number on this page unmeasurable. A bowl that is topped up at seven in the morning and again at six in the evening tells you nothing about what the dog ate, so a 4 percent weight change over a month has no explanation attached to it.

It also erases appetite as a clinical sign. Appetite is one of the first things a veterinarian asks about, and a free-fed Chihuahua has no baseline to compare against. Put the weighed ration down, give it fifteen minutes, lift what is left, and offer it again at the next meal. A healthy adult can miss a meal without harm. Two consecutive missed meals in a dog under three pounds is a call to the clinic, not a reason to add a topper.

Two Chihuahuas sharing one bowl is the same problem with a witness. The smaller dog usually loses. Feed in separate rooms, on the same schedule, with each ration weighed separately.

Ten Years Old and Still Here

A Chihuahua at ten is not an old dog by the breed's clock. Life expectancy runs 14 to 16 years, and plenty reach 18, so the second half of the animal's life is longer than the first and deserves its own portion rather than a continuation of the adult number.

Activity falls before appetite does. A senior carrying the same 1.4 factor it had at four is usually being overfed, and 1.2 to 1.3 is the honest range once the walks shorten. Protein should not come down with it. Senior formulas that cut protein to 18 percent on a dry-matter basis are the wrong direction for a dog losing muscle, and 25 to 30 percent dry matter is the better target.

Hands on the ribs once a week settle the rest, and on a smooth coat the waist is visible from above. A dog that chews on one side or suddenly prefers soft food is describing a sore tooth. The Pet BMI & Ideal Weight Calculator turns a body condition score into a target weight.

Three findings move a Chihuahua off the portion chart and into an exam room. A dog that refuses food for 36 hours, because a four-pound animal has almost no reserve and dehydration arrives faster than it does in a retriever. Weight loss on a ration that is actually being weighed, which points at the mouth, the gut, or the thyroid rather than the scoop. And a two-pound adult that drops four ounces, which is 12 percent of the animal and the equivalent of a Labrador losing nine pounds.

Everything else is a scale, a number on a lid, and a bowl that gets filled twice a day with something measured in it.
