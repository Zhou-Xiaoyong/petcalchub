Is Your Vet Open on Thanksgiving? The 90-Hour Gap

By the PetCalcHub Editorial Team · Published November 12, 2026 · 8 min read

Most general practices close at 5 p.m. on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving and pick up the phone again at 11 a.m. on Monday. That is ninety hours with no regular vet in it.

Fourteen days from today the bird goes in the oven. The piece of the holiday worth planning before then is not the food. It is where you would drive at 2 a.m., what that drive costs, and whether anyone would pay you back afterward.

Quick answer: Your clinic is almost certainly closed Thursday and likely Friday. The places that are open are 24/7 emergency hospitals, where the exam alone runs $100 to $250 before a holiday premium of 20 to 50 percent. Poison control is $85 to $95 and hands you a case number an ER vet will treat from. What you need this week is three phone numbers, one address, and a card with real headroom on it.

Ninety Hours, and the Last Usable Appointment Is Tuesday

Wednesday at five to Monday at eleven is four nights. A clinic that keeps those hours is not being difficult. It runs on the same holiday calendar as every other small business, and the dog does not read the calendar.

The Wednesday before Thanksgiving is a boarding and refill day. Kennel drop-offs stack up in the morning, prescriptions get called in all afternoon, and the schedule fills with people asking to be squeezed in. A dog that starts vomiting at three tends to get offered Monday, and the reason is the calendar rather than the case.

So Tuesday is the real deadline for anything borderline. A limp that has been there since the weekend. A cough. Soft stool. A lump somebody noticed in the shower. Book it Tuesday, because by Wednesday afternoon the answer is the emergency hospital, and that answer costs five times more.

Get the hours off the recorded message, not memory, and listen to the end. Most clinics name the hospital that covers them overnight.

The hotline is the cheapest line on that table and the only one that ends with a written protocol. The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center charges $95 per case at (888) 426-4435. Pet Poison Helpline charges $85 per incident at (855) 764-7661. Both run around the clock, both include follow-up calls on the same case, and both hand you a case number. Give that number to the emergency vet and the toxicologist's arithmetic comes with it, which is the difference between treating a dose and guessing at one.

Pick by situation:
  • Something swallowed within the last two hours: hotline first, then drive.
  • A limp, a cut, an ear, a swollen eye: urgent care, if it is open before ten.
  • A hard belly, no urine, white gums, labored breathing: emergency hospital now, no call ahead.

A poisoning visit that ends with induced vomiting, charcoal and four hours of monitoring usually lands between $300 and $800. Hospitalization is what turns that into four figures, and a holiday premium lands on most line items rather than one.

What Cannot Wait Until Monday

Six things. A dog with a hard, distended belly that retches and brings nothing up, which is a twisted stomach and is measured in hours. A male cat in and out of the box producing a drop or nothing, which can turn fatal in a day or two as potassium climbs. A seizure still running at five minutes, or three in one day. Gums that are white where they should be pink. Breathing that is work while the dog is lying still, especially in a flat-faced breed in a warm kitchen with the oven on. A bitch straining hard for an hour without a puppy.

The other column is longer than most owners expect. One vomit, and the dog is drinking and asking to go out: Monday. A mild limp that eases with rest: Monday. A clean cut that stopped bleeding on its own: Monday. Soft stool with no blood and no vomiting: Monday. Monday means the first appointment of the day, not the afternoon.

The low column adds to $4,500 and the high column to $12,700. Two mechanics matter more than the total. The first is the deposit: most emergency hospitals want 50 to 100 percent of the low estimate before surgery starts, so ask for the estimate in writing and ask which lines are optional. Imaging and bloodwork sometimes are. An open airway is not. The second is that the estimate arrives as a range while the deposit is one number you produce at the desk.

Against an annual food budget of roughly $600, one bloat surgery is seven years of kibble. That comparison is why the insurance question comes up every November at the wrong hour.

The Policy You Buy Today Covers Illness on November 26

Fourteen days from November 12 is Thanksgiving Day. A policy with the standard 14-day illness waiting period, bought this evening, clears that clock on the morning of the 26th. Depending on the carrier, that is either excellent timing or the last day the arithmetic works.

The other clocks are less friendly. Accident coverage starts somewhere between the next morning and 14 days out. Orthopedic conditions such as cruciate tears and hip dysplasia wait up to six months on some plans. A curable pre-existing condition comes back after roughly 180 symptom-free days. A chronic one never comes back at all.

Then the part that surprises people at the counter. Insurance reimburses, it does not pay. You settle the bill, submit it, and the money arrives days to weeks later. At 2 a.m. the limit is your available credit, not your policy number. The pet insurance cost calculator returns a monthly premium for your species, age and coverage level. Multiply by twelve and hold that against the $4,500 invoice above before deciding a policy is the answer.

The Page You Write This Week

One sheet on the refrigerator. Your clinic's holiday hours, copied from the recording. The name and address of the nearest 24/7 hospital, and the second nearest, because the first one may be full at midnight. Both hotline numbers. Your dog's weight in pounds and in kilograms, since every dose is figured from it. Current medications and the time of the last dose. A photo of the rabies certificate in your phone. The name of the person who drives.

Thanksgiving Vet Questions

Is my vet open on Thanksgiving Day?

Assume not. Most general practices close Thursday, and a large share stay closed Friday as well. Emergency hospitals are open every day of the year and price accordingly. Call your clinic's number today and listen to the message rather than guessing from last year.

My dog seems fine after eating something it should not have. Can it wait until Monday?

The quiet stretch is the trap, not the reassurance. Most poisonings look like nothing for four to twelve hours, and decontamination works best inside the first two. If you know roughly what went in and how much, spend the $85 to $95 and let a toxicologist run the math against your dog's weight.

Will the emergency hospital bill my insurance directly?

Usually not. Most US policies reimburse you after you pay, so bring a card that works. A few carriers have direct-pay arrangements with specific hospitals. Worth knowing before the holiday which yours is.

Ninety hours is long enough to lose a dog to something that had a two-hour fix, and long enough to do nothing about it because the clinic was closed and nobody had written down where to go instead.

Drive now, without calling ahead, for a hard distended belly with unproductive retching, a cat that cannot pass urine, a seizure past five minutes, white gums, or breathing that is work at rest. Call the hotline first for anything swallowed. Everything else can wait for the first appointment on Monday, and that appointment is worth booking before you sit down to dinner.
